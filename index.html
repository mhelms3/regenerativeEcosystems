Implementation roadmap

Advance only when each decision gate is satisfied.

The roadmap separates research, pre-feasibility, pilots, commercial deployment, and long-term stewardship.

  1. 0
    Alignment & framing0–30 days

    Confirm objectives, partners, site constraints, community priorities, material flows, and data requests.

  2. 1
    Pre-feasibility31–90 days

    Test resource availability, markets, permits, land, operators, capital needs, and integration dependencies.

  3. 2
    Concept design & agreements3–6 months

    Select site and pilot package; draft heat, water, land, feedstock, community-benefit, and governance terms.

  4. 3
    Instrumented pilot6–18 months

    Build modular demonstrations with metering, quality systems, workforce programs, public reporting, and stop/go criteria.

  5. 4
    Scale & stewardship18+ months

    Deploy bankable operations, restore connected landscapes, verify benefits, and fund long-term maintenance.

Resource gate

Reliable heat, water, feedstock, land, or ecological resource at required quantity and quality.

Technical gate

Process works at expected scale, temperature, throughput, quality, and operating conditions.

Market gate

Qualified internal user or buyer, realistic price, specifications, and logistics.

Permitting gate

Credible approvals path for solid waste, water, air, wetlands, food, fire, and local land use.

Land gate

Long-term site control, access, easements, environmental due diligence, and expansion rights.

Finance gate

Complete capital stack, operating model, contingencies, reserves, and downside case.

Operator gate

Qualified owner/operator with staffing, safety, maintenance, quality, and reporting capacity.

Community gate

Documented priorities, enforceable benefits, burden controls, grievance path, and reporting.

Environmental gate

No unacceptable impacts, contamination pathways, habitat losses, emissions, or water risks.

Integration gate

Interfaces are metered, contracted, resilient to outages, and governed across entities.

Evidence architecture

Connect public communication to auditable methods.

The public-facing four-pillar model should sit on top of a more rigorous benefit-accounting stack.

Integrated approach

One public model, multiple analytical layers

Use the four value pillars for communication. Use specialized frameworks behind them to define boundaries, metrics, dependencies, beneficiaries, and verification.

  • Avoid counting the same benefit under multiple pillars.
  • Track both positive outcomes and project burdens.
  • Separate outputs, outcomes, and long-term impacts.
  • Assign each metric one accountable owner and one source of record.
